Most of the runners I coach come to me with the same worry: "If I lift, won't I get heavy and slow?" The short answer is no. Two or three short strength sessions a week make you more resilient and more efficient, especially late in a race.

## 1. The back squat

Strong quads and glutes keep your form together when you're tired, which is when most injuries happen. Start light, keep it controlled and build up over weeks.

## 2. The Romanian deadlift

Your hamstrings and glutes drive you up hills. The RDL trains them through a long range without loading your back the way a heavy conventional deadlift does.

## 3. Single-leg work

Running is a series of single-leg hops. Split squats and step-ups expose and fix the differences between your left and right side.
